Verification

The solver is held to analytic theory and to the literature by the studies in verification/, one script each. Every study has a page of its own under Verification in the navigation: the script's code, the numbers it prints and the figures it draws. This page says what the test suite asserts about them, and gathers the measurements behind each claim, study by study.

Running the studies

Each study executes directly and writes its figures beside itself. The scripts are written in Literate.jl comment form, which is how these pages render them; .github/workflows/Scripts.yml runs every one to completion, and every published build of this site runs them again:

julia --project=verification -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'   # once, Julia ≥ 1.11
julia --project=verification verification/landau-damping-1d1v.jl
julia --project=verification verification/plasma-oscillations-1d1v.jl
julia --project=verification verification/two-stream.jl
julia --project=verification verification/bump-on-tail.jl
julia --project=verification verification/plasma-echo.jl
julia --project=verification verification/bgk-equilibrium.jl
julia --project=verification verification/collisional-damping.jl
julia --project=verification verification/wakefield.jl
julia --project=verification verification/scheme-comparison.jl
  • landau-damping-1d1v.jl: linear Landau damping, the recurrence of its modes, and the strong case at α = 0.5, on uniform and non-uniform velocity grids.
  • plasma-oscillations-1d1v.jl: the Bohm–Gross frequency, and the energy to t = 3000 on both grids.
  • two-stream.jl: the growth rate across the unstable branch, against the warm kinetic root.
  • bump-on-tail.jl: Arber and Vann's beam, through saturation by trapping.
  • plasma-echo.jl: the echo with the field off and on, and what four schemes keep of it.
  • bgk-equilibrium.jl: a nonlinear stationary state, and where the solver moves it.
  • collisional-damping.jl: BGK in the loop, against its own dispersion relation.
  • wakefield.jl: a laser driving a plasma wave, against linear theory.
  • scheme-comparison.jl: the advection schemes ranked on the Landau rate and on positivity. It is advisory and exits 0.

Trapping, reversibility, the Doppler shift, convergence and the invariants are runs of the test suite alone, with no study of their own.

What the tests assert

The studies' headline claims are asserted by the test suite rather than left in prose. CI runs this on every pull request; locally it is behind an environment variable so that a default Pkg.test() stays instant:

VASILEK_EXTENDED=1 julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.test()'

The analytic side of those claims is computed rather than quoted: test/dispersion.jl solves the kinetic dispersion relation through the plasma dispersion function, which gives the Landau roots at any k — they used to be three constants typed into the test file — the growth rate of warm counter-streaming beams, which is what the runs contain, and the growing root of the bump-on-tail distribution, on the continuum and on the grid's own centred-difference field. The cold two-stream closed form remains as its zero-temperature limit and is checked as such. With the BGK operator in the loop, the same Z gives the collisional relation: a 3×3 closure on the three moments the operator restores, checked against the Landau root at ν = 0, the Krook model's closed form, and the Chapman–Enskog fluid at large ν. Z(ζ) = i√π·erfcx(−iζ) is written through the entire Faddeeva function, so one expression covers the whole plane, and test/test_dispersion.jl holds it to identities rather than to stored numbers: Z against its own Cauchy integral where that converges, to 3.5e-15, and the susceptibility against a trapezoid that never calls Z, to 1.3e-14.

Every row below is asserted in test/test_verification.jl, the extended suite, except the field-off echo's four rows, in test/VlasovSolver/test_echo.jl, and the collisional relation, in test/test_dispersion.jl, which run in a default Pkg.test().

Measurements by study

Each section says what is claimed, what measures it, and the numbers. The measurement journals these were gathered from, as they stood through commit f5032f9, are in the git history of CHANGELOG.md.

Landau damping and dispersion

One k with one fitted γ is a single point on a curve, and cannot separate a solver that reproduces the dispersion relation from one that lands near a value at one wavenumber. Both roots are measured at k = 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5 against landau_root, which solves the kinetic dispersion relation rather than quoting it (testset "Landau damping and dispersion"; damping_rate, oscillation_frequency and trapping_phase in the harness). The real frequency is four to nine times the sharper of the two, since it comes from counting nulls where γ comes from fitting an amplitude that numerical dissipation also acts on, so it is held to 1% where γ is held to 3%.

Three constraints shape the runs, and each cost time to rediscover:

  • The velocity window must contain the resonance at v = ω/k (3.87, 3.21, 2.83), since that is where the damping comes from; the window then fixes Δt, because the fastest row runs at max|v|·Δt/Δx against the scheme's Courant limit. The k = 0.5 case runs at 0.815.
  • The fitting window stops before the floor where recurrence and round-off take over, and the floor arrives earlier in e-foldings the weaker the damping, which is why each k has its own window; at α = 1e-2, where k = 0.3 had decayed only threefold by t = 50, fitting past it read γ 22% low.
  • The runs are linear. At α = 1e-2 the k = 0.3 case is not: trapping stops its damping inside the fitting window (next section), and the γ it reported sat below the root, two errors cancelling, where dissipation can only push it high. At α = 1e-3 the rate is flat to t = 95; the fitting window is [10, 90], thirty maxima, and the bounce phase trapping_phase reports over each window, 1.70, 0.45 and 0.21 at k = 0.3, 0.4 and 0.5, is asserted under 2.

damping_rate fits a line through the maxima of log ε_e only. Fitted through every sample, the log cos² nulls pull the slope by an amount that depends on where the window starts; that is what once read 0.1498 at k = 0.5. Both estimators are held to their windows: two windows agree in γ to 0.17%, 0.05% and 0.04%, and in ω to 0.066%, 0.000% and 0.300%. ω's 1% sits in a gap. Below them is the estimator's own floor: a null is located only to within Δt, so two windows disagree by about Δt/span whatever the physics does, 0.4% at k = 0.5. Above them is the failure tested for: losing one null of ten rescales the spacing by 11%.

The study renders the k = 0.5 run's spectrum, which peaks at 1.4137 against the root's 1.4157.

Trapping and the bounce time

The linear cases are linear because their amplitude was chosen to make them so. At larger amplitudes trapped particles stop the damping, which is O'Neil's result, and reproducing when it happens is a statement about the nonlinear term that no damped-mode test can make (testset "Trapping stops the damping, on the bounce time", at k = 0.3). At α = 1e-2 the local rate between maxima two apart holds near 0.013 to t ≈ 30, reaches zero at t ≈ 73 and goes negative after it, the field growing again as the trapped population sloshes; at α = 1e-3 the same rate stays between 0.0121 and 0.0129 to t = 95.

With ω_B = √(kE₀) = √α here, the arrest time should go as α^(−1/2), and the bounce phase ω_B·t₀ at which it happens should not move. The arrest time is interpolated between the last positive local rate and the first non-positive one, so it is not quantised to the spacing of the maxima:

Recurrence

On a discrete velocity grid a phase-mixed mode does not stay mixed: the sheared modes rephase, and the mode returns at T_R = 2π/(kΔv), a time set by Δv alone that no scheme can move. In the Landau run the seeded k = 0.5 mode returns at 128.6 against 125.7, at 52% of its initial amplitude, and the k = 1 harmonic it generates returns at 64.3 against 62.8, from a round-off floor of 6.25e-17; over t ∈ [58, 72] the harmonic is 21 times the seeded mode (testset "Each mode recurs at its own 2π/(kΔv)"; the driver records any modes asked of it through modes). What the Landau study's figure shows near t = 63 is therefore the harmonic's recurrence, not the fundamental's.

With the field off the Vlasov equation has an exact solution, and the same statement is made in test/VlasovSolver/test_free_streaming.jl: a modulated Maxwellian's density mode decays as exp(−k²t²/2), a Gaussian rather than an exponential, because the mode is sheared into velocity structure rather than dissipated. Over t ≤ 4 the mode amplitude is off by 2.7e-6 for cubic SemiLagrangian, 3.3e-5 for PFC, 1.2e-3 for LaxWendroff and 1.7e-2 for Upwind, and the ordering is asserted. On a coarse velocity grid, Δv = 0.4 and T_R = 31.4159, cubic SemiLagrangian and PFC peak at t = 31.42, recovering 1.0000 and 0.9991 of the initial amplitude. The Courant limit binds here as it does not in one-dimensional advection: the fastest velocity row carries max|v|·Δt/Δx, and everything runs at 0.611.

The plasma echo

The echo is the one place a kinetic theory beyond linear order is held to a number. A seed at k₁ = 1, a velocity kick at k₂ = 3/2 when t = 5, and around t = 15 a mode nobody seeded appears at k₃ = 1/2: phase mixing is reversible, and the echo is made of information no moment of f can see. So it measures what a scheme keeps of filaments finer than any it can show, which free streaming, measuring only the decay, never did.

Field off (test/VlasovSolver/test_echo.jl, fast suite). The echo has a closed form, exact in both amplitudes, −iα·J₁(k₃ε(t − τ))·exp(−(k₃t − k₂τ)²/2) (echo_closed_form in test/echo.jl). The Bessel function is not a refinement: its small-argument limit is 14.6% high at these amplitudes. The sign is asserted separately, since the direction of the kick changes it and nothing about the peak. PFC at 128 × 241 follows the closed form to 1.25e-3 of the peak, pointwise, out of a seeded mode the kick found at 1.84e-6, 2.4e4 times smaller than the echo it turns into. The loss converges at orders 2.89 and 2.78 as Δv halves, and, at 64 × 121, it ranks the schemes: cubic SemiLagrangian 1.07e-3, PFC 1.03e-2, LaxWendroff 4.48e-2, and Upwind 0.449, which returns 55% of the echo and loses most of the rest in the x-sweep rather than in the kick.

Field on (testset "The plasma echo, with the plasma's own field", extended suite). echo_second_order composes three linear responses of the Maxwellian: the seed's filament screened at k₁, the kick screened at k₂ together with the lifetime of the field it induces, and the echo's own density polarising the plasma at k₃, solved as a Volterra equation. Its pieces are checked before any run is held to it: the resonant dielectric function is dielectric to 1.2e-16, the filament's closed form agrees with a time-domain solve to 4.9e-6, and with the field off the theory is the closed form's small-ε limit to 1.1e-14. The self-consistent run (self_consistent_echo, 128 × 241, α = ε = 0.01, τ = 10) follows it to 4.8e-3 of the peak, pointwise, the ringing after the peak included, with the peak 0.48% low on the same sample, t = 29.05. The field-off form would put that peak 2.1 times higher and 1.14 later. The residual is the x-sweep's: 1.6e-2 at Nx = 64, where halving Δv or Δt instead leaves it at 1.5e-2 and 1.7e-2.

The BGK equilibrium

The equilibrium study is the converse of every other run: it starts on a nonlinear stationary state and asks how little the solver moves it. The state is a function of the particle energy in the potential −ψ cos kx, on an ion background built to hold it (bgk_equilibrium in the harness; the driver keeps the ions it is handed as nᵢ). At ψ = 0.5 two thirds of the particles are trapped, so this is also the first run whose physics they carry (testset "A nonlinear equilibrium stays put, and gives on the separatrix").

The Maxwell–Boltzmann equilibrium, analytic across the separatrix, holds f to 1.52e-3 of its peak and the field to 3.85e-3 through t = 50. That is a steady drift, not an oscillation about the equilibrium, and it is the scheme's dissipation: L² falls by 1.13e-3 and the entropy rises by 1.27e-4, where the exact flow keeps both. The error spreads over phase space and converges at PFC's third order, 7.6 times less in f and 6.1 in the field per halving; halving Δt alone moves neither.

Give the trapped particles a temperature of their own and F keeps its value but not its slope across the separatrix. The worst cell is then on the separatrix at both resolutions, where the smooth case's is 13 and 24 cells away, the error is twelve times the smooth one, and it converges at first order, 1.75 per halving. Trapped-particle structure lives or dies there.

The teeth: ions built for a potential 10% off put the field 49% and 59% off at once, and f drifts 24 and 27 times further; ions built on the Poisson sign the normalization notes once gave hold the reversed field, E/E₀ = −1.000, and the run leaves the equilibrium by 16% of its peak.

A trapped population colder than the bulk peaks at 1.79, above the f = 1 the harness once bounded PFC by. Bounded by f₀, as every run now is, it holds to 0.99% of its peak. At the old bound the limiter, handed data above its fmax, turned inside out and took f 43.6% of its peak away, without an error. PFCNonUniform now checks its data as PFC does and refuses that bound on the first call; built unchecked, it reproduces the 43.6%, and the extended suite asserts both. Over the two-stream runs carried into saturation and the three equilibria, 3.8 million calls of the checked schemes stay inside their bounds, sub-steps included.

Reversibility

Vlasov–Poisson is invariant under (t, v) → (−t, −v) with E unchanged. Run forward, flip the velocity axis, run forward again, flip back: an exact scheme returns the initial state, and what a real one loses is its own dissipation (testset "The Vlasov-Poisson flow is reversible, and what breaks it"; vlasov_poisson returns f so that the question can be asked of the distribution rather than of a moment of it).

  • At α = 0.05 the round-trip error is 2.82e-3, 6.48e-4 and 1.58e-4 as the grid halves, ratios 4.3 and 4.1: second order, so the irreversibility is the scheme's and converges away. Bounded at the initial maximum, PFC's limiter clips the peak cell, which costs the third order it shows when bounded at 1 (ratios 5.7 and 7.1); the test asks for 3.5.
  • At α = 0.5 it is 0.167 and stays 0.157 when the grid is halved, a factor of 1.06. By then the flow has folded the distribution into filaments finer than Δv, and the information needed to run the film backwards is not on the mesh to be refined. Every nonlinear run in the suite is in that regime by the time it is interesting.
  • Ranked by round trip at α = 0.5: LaxWendroff 0.090, cubic SemiLagrangian 0.108, PFC 0.167, Upwind 0.332. The first two get there by driving f to −0.093 and −0.057 against a peak of 0.6, over the round trip's two runs of t = 20 on 64 × 121 cells, where PFC stays at +5e-10 and Upwind pays a fifth of its L² norm instead. Reversibility and positivity are the same trade-off from two sides, reached through a quantity that has nothing to do with the damping rate the scheme comparison ranks them by.

The same statement with the field off, a rigid rotation run forward, flipped, run forward and flipped back (test/VlasovSolver/test_strang_splitting.jl), separates the schemes far more sharply than the forward error does. At N = 64 → 128: cubic SemiLagrangian 1.01e-3 → 1.23e-4, LaxWendroff 3.77e-3 → 4.94e-4, PFC 1.95e-2 → 3.61e-3, and Upwind 4.04e-1 → 2.56e-1. Upwind is there because it fails: 40% of the peak, smeared past recovery, and refining barely helps because its dissipation is first order.

Strong Landau damping

The α = 0.5 case is the standard nonlinear benchmark, and it is quoted by two numbers: Cheng and Knorr (1976) give γ₁ = −0.281 and γ₂ = 0.084, later work −0.292 with 0.0815 and −0.2918 with 0.08584, the literature quoting the decay with a negative sign. Here γ₁ is the rate of decay, held within 0.27…0.30, and γ₂ the rate of regrowth, within 0.070…0.090 (testset "Strong Landau damping: the damping stops and reverses"). Measured on 128 × 241 with Δt = 0.025: γ₁ = 0.2863 over the four maxima of the decay proper, γ₂ = 0.0787 over the eight of the regrowth. Both windows are conventions, and the testset says so with numbers: γ₁ reads 0.3793 over three maxima and 0.2283 over five, because the envelope steepens and then flattens into the trapping plateau, so a straight line through it depends on how much of the curve is inside the window.

Refinement is what makes the agreement more than a coincidence of one grid: γ₂ goes 0.0714 → 0.0787 → 0.0813 as Δx, Δv and Δt halve, toward the published value rather than away from it. The non-uniform velocity grid, the only path the suite has toward an adaptive mesh, reproduces both rates to 0.06% and 0.5%, on a run that reaches the sharp gradients its limiter exists for.

The Doppler shift

Every other Vlasov–Poisson case starts from a distribution symmetric in v, so the mean velocity is zero throughout and the drifting half of the solver is never exercised. A boost by u carries f(x, v, t) → f(x − ut, v − u, t) and E(x, t) → E(x − ut, t), so the mode's history should be the same complex function times exp(−ikut) (testset "A drifting plasma damps the same way, Doppler-shifted", at u = 0.5). Nothing in the discretisation is Galilean invariant, since the grid does not move and the boosted Maxwellian sits on it asymmetrically, so the agreement is a measurement: over the eleven maxima in the fitting window, amplitudes within 1.7e-3 and phases within 1.8e-3 rad, with fitted rates 0.014% apart and frequencies identical to the estimator's resolution. Without the Doppler correction the phase differs by up to 2.88 rad, which is what says the correction is doing work.

The phase is compared at each sample's own time, t[k] + Δt/2, where the driver records the field (driver notes); removing the Doppler factor at t[k] instead reads 8.0e-3, of which 6.25e-3 is that half-step and not the grid. The rates agree to 0.014% because the energies are the cell-width sums the schemes conserve; the trapezoid, which books a travelling wave's passage across the seam as energy, put them 0.136% apart. The comparison is made at the maxima rather than pointwise: both |E_k| and ε_e pass through deep nulls, and two nulls landing a time step apart read as a 900% discrepancy.

Convergence under refinement

Agreement at one resolution can be a coincidence of that grid; the testset "Landau damping converges under refinement" asks that it improve.

In the Landau run, halving Δx, Δv and Δt together, so that the Courant number stays at 0.81 and only the discretisation moves, gives γ errors of 6.47%, 1.33%, 0.71% and 0.47% for Nx = 32 to 256. The L² dissipation over the same ladder falls by 11.0, 7.9 and 7.8 times against the 8 a third-order scheme predicts, which identifies the residual error in γ: the fitted rate is the physical damping plus the scheme's own, which is why every measurement sits on the high side of the root rather than scattering about it. The testset runs the first three rungs and asks that the error fall at each and end under 1%, and that the dissipation fall between 4 and 16 times per halving.

The splitting is measured with a real scheme against an analytic answer in test/VlasovSolver/test_strang_splitting.jl, on a rigid rotation, refining Δx, Δv and Δt together over N = 32, 64, 128: cubic SemiLagrangian goes 6.37e-3 → 5.72e-5 at orders 3.60 and 3.19, PFC converges at 2.26 and 2.22, and LaxWendroff at 1.84 and 1.96. Each scheme keeps its own spatial order rather than being dragged to the splitting's second. That is not the problem being easy: a rigid rotation factors into shears, which is what the splitting does, so the commutator error is unusually small here and the scheme is what is left.

Invariants

Over the k = 0.5 Landau case, 875 steps, mass drifts 1.6e-15 and momentum stays at 2.2e-15 on a mass of 25.1: round-off, both exact conservation laws the discrete scheme also satisfies. L² falls 1.0e-5 and the entropy rises 7.5e-6, monotonically at every step. Those two are not conserved and are not asserted as if they were: an exact Vlasov flow preserves both, and the drift is numerical dissipation, so what is asserted is the direction (testset "The invariants of the split system"; test/test_invariants.jl for the kernels alone).

The invariants and the energies are the cell-width sums Σ f ΔvΔx and Σ e² Δx, the quadrature a flux form conserves, not the trapezoid (driver notes). On the same run the trapezoid reports 2.4e-4 of mass drift and 7.3e-4 of momentum, eleven orders of magnitude of apparent non-conservation that belongs to its endpoint weights. On the bump-on-tail run, whose wave travels across the seam, the trapezoid's ε, 61.8, swung by −0.346 to +0.175 over t ∈ [60, 100] on 64 × 361 cells and by −0.185 to +0.093 at Nx = 128, first order in Δx, where the sums drift by 9.7e-3 and 1.0e-3.

Collisional damping

The collisional study is the first run with the collision operator in it. BGK had been checked on a single velocity line, and never with a field. Put between the kicks at three rates, it lands on its own dispersion relation, which says something one might not guess: collisions that restore density, momentum and energy weaken the damping, from Landau's 0.153 towards a fluid wave that damps only by conducting heat. A relaxation that restored less would damp harder, and the testset ("Collisional Landau damping: BGK in the loop") runs two such relaxations to show that each lands on its own root.

The relation. Linearised, BGK relaxes a mode towards F₀[n₁ + u₁v + T₁(v² − 1)/2], and the three moments close the kinetic equation into a 3×3 determinant built from Z (collisional_determinant, collisional_root and heat_mode_root in test/dispersion.jl). At ν = 0 the determinant is the collisionless dielectric function, to 2.3e-14; restricted to the density it is the Krook model's closed form, to 4.7e-16. At large ν it approaches the Chapman–Enskog fluid, derived separately: no viscosity in one dimension, heat flux −(3nT/ν)∂ₓT, and a cubic whose roots are the Langmuir pair and a heat-conduction mode. The gaps in ω, γ and the heat rate close as 1/ν², 3.85 to 4.08 times per doubling, as the next order of the expansion says they should. Where the root sits above the real axis, at ν ≥ 0.3, a trapezoid over the mode's own moments, which never calls Z, confirms it (2.3e-14). At k = 0.5 the root's γ goes 0.1534 → 0.1327 → 0.1062 → 0.0652 at ν = 0, 0.1, 0.3, 1, towards a wave damped by conduction alone at 3k⁴/(ν(1 + 3k²)) and oscillating at the adiabatic √(1 + 3k²). Restoring fewer moments strengthens the damping: dγ/dν at ν = 0 is −0.243 for BGK, +0.114 without energy and +0.684 for Krook. The collisional roots take a looser secant stop than the collisionless ones, 1e-9 rather than 1e-12: J₃ and J₄ come from 1 + ζJ₂, which cancels when ν is large, and against quadrature they carry 1.2e-10 of their size at ν = 20. heat_mode_root searches the imaginary axis only above Im ζ = −2; further down the continuation of Z grows as exp(|ζ|²/2).

BGK. On 64 × 161 over v ∈ [−8, 8] with Δt = 0.04, each run lands on the root on the grid's field (collisional_landau in the harness): γ reads +0.37%, +0.49% and +0.76% above it at ν = 0.1, 0.3 and 1, ω +0.05%, +0.08% and +0.03%, and the damping falls as ν rises. The residue in γ is the x-sweep's dissipation: halving Δx and Δt at ν = 1 takes it to 0.03%, while halving Δv moves γ by 0.01%.

Partial relaxations. PartialBGK relaxes at the background temperature, restoring only the density (the Krook model) or the density and the drift. Run in place of BGK, each lands on the root collisional_root gives for what it restores, to 0.31% and 0.23% in γ, at 3.3 and 2.1 times BGK's rate. So the relation is checked for three operators, and a BGK that lost its temperature or its drift would fail by a factor.

The heat mode. Energy conservation gives the plasma a third mode at each k, a temperature perturbation that does not oscillate. The density seed excites it at 21% of the wave's amplitude at t = 4, and it decays six times faster than the wave, so the maxima and nulls never see it. A matrix pencil of the field's k mode, mode_exponents, returns the wave pair and a real exponent, 0.43377 against heat_mode_root's 0.43369 at ν = 1; its wave agrees with the maxima and nulls to 0.06% in γ and 0.04% in ω. The pencil takes the left singular vectors: the right ones return every exponent conjugated, which on a test signal swapped a travelling wave's direction and nothing else.

The window. The operator relaxes each line to its discrete Maxwellian, the exp(a + bv + cv²) whose density, momentum and energy on the grid equal the line's, so it conserves all three to round-off on any window. At ν = 1 the energy holds to 8.0e-9 on ±8 and to 8.0e-8 on ±4, and the entropy rises monotonically. The sampled Maxwellian it used before, still there as BGK(τ; conservative = false), took the temperature by the trapezoid over the window and put back a Maxwellian over the whole line, so a window that cut the tail cooled the plasma at every relaxation, by 1.2e-3 of the energy: by t = 60 on ±4 the run has lost 5.3% of its energy, T has fallen to 0.949, and γ reads 3.0% low.

Plasma oscillation frequency and energy

A solver oscillating at entirely the wrong rate passes an energy test without difficulty, so the frequency is measured as well (testsets "Plasma oscillations, Bohm–Gross frequency" and "Plasma oscillations, energy conservation"). At k = 2π/100, ω = 1.005719 against √(1 + 3k²) = 1.005904, 0.018%, where the cold ωₚ = 1 is 0.57% away. The factor of thirty is the point: the test fails if the thermal correction is dropped rather than merely preferring it there. That costs a second at t ≤ 200, where the energy test needs t = 3000 and forty times as long. Two windows agree to 0.006%.

The study renders the spectrum of the mode the driver records, projected on the standing wave's phase and zero-padded; it peaks at 1.00528 against Bohm–Gross's 1.00590.

Over t ≤ 3000 the energy drifts 0.38% on the uniform velocity grid and 4.75% on the non-uniform one, against 0.5% and 6%. The non-uniform figure was about 12% while PFCNonUniform took its limiter from the grid's global spacing ratio rather than each cell's own. Within a plasma period ε moves by 6.6e-5 of itself, asserted under 3e-4, because its kinetic energy is the mean of the two sides of the kick and so is sampled at t[k] + Δt/2 with the field (driver notes). Summed after the kick it swung by 1.2e-3, half the kick's work counted early, and the agreement at t = 3000 was then a point on that swing landing near the drift.

The two-stream instability

Every other case here is a damped or neutral mode. A growth rate catches a class of error damping cannot: a sign flip in the field push turns damping into growth and growth into damping, so a suite of damped cases alone is half-blind to it (testset "Two-stream instability"; two_stream and growth_rate in the harness, two_stream_warm in test/dispersion.jl).

The beams are Maxwellians at ±v₀ with vt = 0.3, and the reference is the warm kinetic root. The cold relation, 1 = ½/(ω − kv₀)² + ½/(ω + kv₀)², is a quadratic in ω², u± = [(2a² + 1) ± √(8a² + 1)]/2 with a = kv₀, unstable exactly when a < 1, with γ = √(−u₋); it stays as the warm root's zero-temperature limit, checked against its relation to 1.4e-14. At a = 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 the runs read −0.63%, −1.95% and +0.09% from the warm root; from the cold one they read −2.10%, −3.15% and +0.30%, half a tolerance spent on a known approximation. γ(a) is not monotone and peaks near a = √(3/8), so reproducing all three is a statement about the branch: a solver that merely amplified what it was given could not put the maximum in the right place.

Finite temperature can make a beam grow faster than a cold one: the warm root crosses above the cold between a = 0.75 and 0.8, and past a = 1 the cold branch is identically zero while warm beams still grow. At a = 1.0 the warm root predicts 0.09823 and the run gives 0.09513, 3.16%, held to 8% because the ripple below is worst where γ is smallest: over the band's upper edge hi ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5} the fit moves between −6.05% and −3.16%. At a = 1.2 and 1.6, where both theories give γ = 0, the mode decays to 0.052 and 0.000 of its initial energy. Doubling the beams' temperature at a = 0.6 lowers γ by 4.4%, where warm theory predicts 4.4%, and each run is within 3% of its own root. The test asserts that relative statement, two temperatures at one wavenumber, rather than any sign at a single wavenumber.

growth_rate fits every sample in a band of amplitude rather than of time: the unstable root is purely imaginary, so the mode grows without oscillating and there are no maxima to fit. That makes it transferable across the branch: at a = 0.4 fixed time windows give 10.65%, 6.43%, 3.99% and 0.19% from the cold root depending on where they are put. ε_e is not one exponential either. The cold quadratic's oscillating pair, ±ω₊, puts a ripple on it whose size relative to the growing mode falls only as exp(−γt): at a = 0.6 the instantaneous rate oscillates with period 4.5 against 2π/ω₊ = 4.626, between 0.21 and 0.41 about a cold γ of 0.353. Fitting over two beat periods rather than one cuts the spread over start points from 41.4%, 14.4% and 22.3% to 9.4%, 5.6% and 4.8%, and the amplitude band already spans 1.15 to 2.22 periods. Under the band, cooling the beams at a = 0.6 from vt = 0.6 to 0.15 brings the rate to the cold one monotonically from below, −7.44% to −2.10%. An exploratory fixed window, t ∈ [10, 20], had it cross the cold value, +0.40% and +0.56% at vt = 0.2 and 0.15: changing vt changes γ slightly, which moves the ripple's phase within a fixed window and drags the fitted rate with it.

The box holds 96 cells by construction, range(Δx; step = Δx, length = Nx): a float range built from its endpoints came up a point short at a = 0.4 and gave the box another fundamental, a = 0.4042. Past their fits the runs grow until the velocity sweep would cross its Courant limit, where the driver splits the step, so they saturate rather than diverge, ε_e peaking at 106 for a = 0.6. Every fit ends at a velocity Courant number of 0.53 to 0.73, before the first split.

The bump-on-tail instability

The bump-on-tail study is the first run held to numbers past its linear phase. The beam is Arber and Vann's [J. Comput. Phys. 180, 339 (2002)], F = [0.9·exp(−v²/2) + 0.2·exp(−2(v − 4.5)²)]/√(2π), over one wavelength of k = 0.3: a Langmuir wave travelling with a weak beam over a warm bulk, where the two-stream case grows as a standing wave. At this beam's temperature the instability is mostly reactive; the temperature cuts the rate by 15% from the cold beam's, and the run has to resolve that cut. Measured on 64 × 361 with Δt = 0.05 (bump_on_tail and mode_rates in the harness; testset "Bump-on-tail: the beam grows a wave, and the wave traps the beam"):

  • Growth. γ = 0.19784 and ω = 1.00108 over t ∈ [30, 50], from a seed of 1e-6: 0.131% and 0.014% below the kinetic root, 1.001218 + 0.198098i. Nearly all of the gap is the Poisson solve's centred difference, which returns sin(kΔx)/(kΔx) of a mode's field. Put that factor on the susceptibilities, as dielectric does given Δx, and the root sits 0.016% and 0.003% from the run. The rate is set in x, not v: Δv from 0.025 to 0.2 moves γ by 0.017% at most, while Nx = 32 puts it 0.69% off the continuum and Nx = 128 0.020%. The phase of the mode amplitude shows the wave travelling with the beam.
  • Saturation. The mode grows until it traps the beam feeding it, and peaks at |E_k| = 0.4979 at t = 74.32, where the bounce frequency is 1.951γ (1.945–1.952 over the grids tried). A seed a thousand times larger peaks at 0.4981, 35.00 earlier, against ln(1000)/γ = 34.91.
  • Trapping. The trapped beam swings round the well and the field swings with it: past the peak the amplitude falls to 0.2645 and comes back to 0.4137, with a period of 21.40, 1.32 bounce periods at the bottom of the well. The averaged distribution loses the slope the growth ran on: the steepest slope of the x-averaged f on the beam's flank falls from 0.096 to 0.0048 by t = 120. It rises and falls with the swing, from 0.0013 to 0.026 over t ∈ [80, 160], so the test asks only for a factor of 3.
  • f stays a distribution function. Its minimum is 3.6e-23, its mass holds to 3.4e-16, and its maximum stays below the initial one.

The seed is not the literature's 0.04: there the mode starts at a quarter of its saturated amplitude, and there is no linear phase to fit. The default suite checks bump_on_tail_root against a quadrature of the formula exactly as Arber and Vann write it, which never calls Z and leaves 5.2e-15 (a beam twice as narrow would leave 0.21). It cools the beam and follows the root to the cold-beam fluid limit, which it meets to 0.026%, and it finds the band's edge at k = 0.4824, where the phase velocity equals the bottom of the valley in F to the last digit, as Penrose's criterion requires.

The laser wakefield

Before the ponderomotive coupling was added, the wakefield example was asserted only to run and stay bounded, because that was all it could support: without the coupling the laser never entered the longitudinal push, and the wake it drew was the slab edges relaxing. Its numbers came out bit-identical whether the transverse current was right or wrong by thirty-two orders of magnitude. The coupling, the force −∂Φ/∂x with Φ = (pʸ² + pᶻ²)/2 in the momentum advection, is now there, and the wakefield rows of the table under What the tests assert measure the wake it produces against linear wakefield theory rather than against a bound (wakefield, linear_wake, vg_pulse and wake_wavelength in the harness; testset "Laser wakefield: the laser drives a plasma wave").

The transverse momentum needed no repair, only a name. Nothing depends on y or z, so p⊥ + A⊥ is conserved along a trajectory and the plasma is at rest ahead of the pulse; with E⊥ = −∂A⊥/∂t the accumulation p⊥ += E⊥Δt is the canonical p⊥ = −A⊥, exact, not a force integral missing v×B. Three things had to be fixed before the wake could be measured: laser_amplitude scaled nothing, the pulse was injected by its tail, its peak due at t = −30.8, before the run began, and laser_duration = 5·2π was k_pσ = 5.7, which suppresses a wake by seven orders of magnitude. The defaults now are laser_duration = 2π, for k_pσ = 1.13; laser_amplitude = 0.3, so that the 1/γ the non-relativistic Φ drops is worth 3.2%; plasma_temperature = 0.01, to keep the edge sheaths off the measurement; and total_time = 2π·22, for enough wake to fit through.

linear_wake integrates the driven oscillator on the Φ the run recorded rather than on an idealised envelope, so the comparison assumes nothing about the shape the laser arrives with, and it shares no code with the wake: Φ comes from the FDTD side, ex from the Vlasov push and the Poisson solve. Its 3T term is load-bearing; at ten cells per wavelength, dropping the thermal correction took the pointwise agreement from 8.9% to 26%. Δε/ε, 13.7%, is supposed to rise: ε is the longitudinal kinetic and electrostatic energy, so a wake trading one for the other leaves it alone, but the transverse motion and field are outside it, and a laser doing work on the plasma pushes energy across that boundary.

The study runs at twenty cells per laser wavelength, and that is a physics choice rather than a taste. At the ten it used before, the Yee dispersion relation puts the carrier's group velocity 4.5% below √(1−n), and the pulse, averaged over its spectrum, 5.2% below, 6.6% as measured: the pulse arrives late and the wake keeps station with it, so the wake's phase velocity is wrong by as much, γ_φ ≈ 2.2 where the physics gives 3.0. The pulse's own bandwidth is worth only half a percent: the continuum group velocity averaged over its spectrum is 0.9438 against 0.9487. vg_pulse, the group velocity of the discrete relation averaged over the same spectrum, predicts 0.8993 at ten cells against 0.8858 measured, and 0.9329 at twenty against 0.9261. None of the assertions about the wake's frequency could see that, since the plasma sets it, and the comparison against linear_wake cannot either, since that reference is driven by the Φ of the run it is checking. It took a closed form for the driver and a second resolution. The wake at twenty cells is 39% larger than at ten, the most resolution-sensitive number in the study.

The a₀² law is tested where the signal dominates: continued to a₀ = 0.075 the ratio reads 3.80 and 3.33 at the two resolutions, because the unlit control's 3.4e-4 is 43% of that wake. Two approximations remain, and the defaults are chosen to stay inside them rather than to be impressive: the ponderomotive potential is the non-relativistic one and the transverse current is taken through momentum rather than velocity, both corrections of relative order p⊥², 3.2% at a₀ = 0.3. test/test_em_plasma.jl holds the transverse half alone to the plasma's discrete dispersion relation, to 3.2e-4 over two densities, three Courant numbers and four wavenumbers, with its cutoff and the sign of its current. See the docstring on wakefield.

The scheme comparison

The comparison of the advection schemes is made on the physics rather than on a shifted sine, and is advisory rather than asserted (verification/scheme-comparison.jl, which exits 0; the timing column is wall clock on whatever machine runs it).

Ranked by error in the Landau damping rate at k = 0.5, LaxWendroff (0.67%) and cubic SemiLagrangian (1.09%) lead the schemes that dissipate, ahead of PFC (1.33%) and Godunov with VanLeer (1.45%), and upwind trails at 48.8%, with Godunov(PiecewiseConstant()) and linear SemiLagrangian, identical to it: its own dissipation is two orders of magnitude larger than the damping it is measuring. A 1% perturbation cannot rank the schemes on positivity, since every one returns the same min f = 1.3e-4, the Maxwellian's tail at v = ±4. At 50% amplitude the two that lead are exactly the two that drive f negative, LaxWendroff to −0.093 and cubic SemiLagrangian to −0.105 against a peak of 0.6, over one run to t = 40 on the round trip's 64 × 121 grid: Godunov's theorem arriving in the physics, and the reason the solvers default to PFC. Superbee heads the table, at 0.21% in the rate and 0.14% in the frequency, from below: its compression anti-diffuses, it shows the only growing L² in the table, +8.9e-5, and its lead is a cancellation. The ranking follows the schemes' order on the sine, over five decades, and collapses on the discontinuity to a factor of four between best and worst. The two second-order schemes change places there: LaxWendroff is linear and so, by Godunov's theorem, cannot be monotone; it rings, and falls behind the limited Godunov, whose limiter buys monotonicity with the extrema it clips on the sine. No ordering of these survives a change of problem class. Upwind, Godunov(PiecewiseConstant()) and linear SemiLagrangian agree to 2.9e-14 after 1280 steps, as the amplification factor they share requires (test/test_amplification.jl, where six schemes are held mode by mode to the symbols of their update formulas, to 1.8e-14, and the dissipation and phase error per wavenumber follow from the same symbols: at λ = 4Δx the phase error is 2.4% for PFC against 29% for LaxWendroff).

Superbee, r -> max(0, min(2r, 1), min(r, 2)) [Roe, Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 18, 337 (1986)], is the upper edge of Sweby's region, the most compressive limiter that keeps Godunov(PiecewiseLinear()) second order and total-variation diminishing to |c| = 1. It is TVD from c = 0.5 to 1 in both directions, the pulse's total-variation ratio between 0.99999999 and 1. It is second order in L¹ but gets there late: the local slopes from N = 32 to 2048 are 1.19, 1.81, 1.91, 1.96, 1.98 and 1.99, and test/test_convergence.jl holds it to 2 over 128 to 1024, where it measures 1.95. Compression has a price the TVD property does not show: under Superbee the L² norm of a smooth perturbation grows, by 1.3e-3 on the sine and 1.2e-2 on the gaussian after a traversal at N = 128, where every other scheme's shrinks, and test/test_invariants.jl asserts the sign.

benchmark/workprecision.jl (advisory) puts error against the cost of reaching it and prints the frontier per problem class, the schemes no other beats on both axes. On smooth data that is Upwind, LaxWendroff, PFC and cubic SemiLagrangian, from 8.1e-3 down to 2.5e-8. On the gaussian and the pulse the limited Godunov joins it: with VanLeer it reaches the pulse's 2.77e-2, a little behind PFC's 2.65e-2, at under a third of PFC's cost, and with Superbee 1.58e-2. Upwind and Godunov(PiecewiseConstant()) are the same scheme, so the gap between their timings is the report's own error bar.
