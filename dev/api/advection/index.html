Vasilek.Advection

Vasilek.Advection — Module
Advection

One-dimensional advection schemes, as types.

Each scheme is an immutable value and advect!(dest, src, scheme, c, ws) dispatches on it. Scratch memory, where a scheme needs any, is an explicit argument from workspace, one per task, so a scheme value can be shared by every line of a multidimensional sweep and by every thread. Options are types (PiecewiseLinear(), VanLeer(), …), so an invalid one fails where it is written. Boundaries are periodic.

Vasilek.Advection.AbstractAdvection1D — Type
AbstractAdvection1D

A one-dimensional advection scheme. Advance one step with

advect!(dest, src, scheme, c)
advect!(dest, src, scheme, c, ws)

where c is the Courant number and ws is scratch from workspace. Boundaries are periodic throughout. Every scheme but SemiLagrangian needs |c| ≤ 1, and advect! refuses more; see _validate_courant.

The fourth argument is a Courant number for every scheme but PFCNonUniform, which takes a displacement, since a non-uniform grid has no single Courant number. OnGrid hides the difference: OnGrid(scheme, Δz) is the scheme on the grid of cell widths Δz, takes a displacement whatever scheme takes, refuses a uniform scheme on a non-uniform grid, and splits a step wider than a cell.

Vasilek.Advection.Godunov — Type
Godunov(reconstruction, limiter = NoLimiter())

Finite-volume scheme with the given interface reconstruction and flux limiter: reconstruct f in each cell, carry the reconstruction exactly for one step, and average it back onto the cells.

The flux through an interface is the upwind cell's reconstruction averaged over the strip that crosses the interface in one step. For PiecewiseLinear and c > 0 (the other direction is the mirror image) the strip's midpoint lies cΔx/2 upwind of the interface, which is (1 − c)Δx/2 downwind of the upwind cell's centre, so

Φᵢ₋½ = c·(fᵢ₋₁ + φ(r)(1 − c)(fᵢ − fᵢ₋₁)/2),    r = (fᵢ₋₁ − fᵢ₋₂)/(fᵢ − fᵢ₋₁),

which is Sweby's flux-limited Lax–Wendroff [Sweby, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 21 (5), 995 (1984)]. φ = 1 gives LaxWendroff and φ = 0 gives Upwind. VanLeer and Superbee keep φ(r) ≤ 2 and φ(r)/r ≤ 2, which makes the scheme total-variation diminishing for every |c| ≤ 1. At |c| = 1 the correction vanishes and the step is a one-cell shift to within an ulp; see _validate_courant.

A cell takes the difference of the fluxes through its two faces, f + (Φin − Φout), so a constant line stays constant to the bit.

The (1 − c) factor is what makes the scheme second order; 0.1's :Riemann_linear lacked it and was first order, and TVD only to |c| ≤ 1/2. A limiter given with PiecewiseConstant() would have no slope to act on and is refused.

Vasilek.Advection.OnGrid — Type
OnGrid(scheme, Δz; scale = maximum(abs, Δz))
OnGrid(scheme::PFCNonUniform[, Δz])

scheme on the grid whose cell widths are Δz. An OnGrid is itself a scheme, and its fourth advect! argument is always a displacement, a length, whatever scheme takes:

  • a scheme that takes a Courant number gets the displacement divided by the grid's spacing, its first width, and is refused on a non-uniform grid, rather than given one of its spacings and left wrong by the ratio between them – an ArgumentError. Uniform is to rounding, as make_poisson takes it, _nonuniform_at: each width matches the first to 1e-10 of it, or to 8 ulps of scale in scale's type. By default that is the widths' own rounding, scale the widest of them. Widths taken from coordinates carry the coordinates' rounding instead, so pass the largest coordinate, in its own type, as vlasov_poisson does: OnGrid(scheme, cell_widths(x); scale = maximum(abs, x)). The grid's extent as the default took a Float32 grid of 4096 cells to 2.5e-3 of its spacing, and a cell 5e-4 wider than the rest with it;
  • PFCNonUniform takes the displacement as it is. It carries its grid, so Δz may be left out; given, it must be that grid, to 1e-12 of each width or 4 ulps of the scheme's own type, so that a Float32 scheme built from a Float64 grid's widths takes that grid. scale is unused.

An OnGrid given again with a grid, OnGrid(og, Δz), is og if Δz is its grid to the same rounding, and refused otherwise.

A step wider than a cell – the narrowest one, for PFCNonUniform – is split into the fewest equal sub-steps that fit, substeps, since advect! refuses it whole and a translation by α is m translations by α/m. A scheme with no Courant limit, SemiLagrangian, takes it in one step; see nsubsteps. Every step a scheme takes on its own is one step here, to the bit: within a cell m = 1, and α/1 is α.

workspace(og, n[, T]) is scratch for the scheme and a line buffer for the sub-steps; n is the grid's length. Its type is the grid's unless T is given, not the data's: Float64 data on a Float32 grid needs workspace(og, n, Float64). advect! refuses a workspace narrower than the data, whose sub-steps it rounded to it, 8.9e-8 off at three sub-steps; the four-argument advect! builds one in the data's type.

Vasilek.Advection.PFC — Type
PFC(; fmin, fmax, checked = true)

Positive Flux Conservative scheme on a uniform grid.

fmin and fmax are required and must bracket the data: the limiter is built on them, and data outside them is corrupted rather than clipped. By Liouville's theorem f stays within the extrema of the initial condition, so pass the global initial bounds; they cannot be derived per line inside a sweep. There is deliberately no default.

With checked = true every call verifies that src lies inside [fmin, fmax] to round-off and throws a DomainError otherwise, and clamps what it returns into [fmin, fmax], at the cost of one minimum/maximum pass and a clamp. The limiter keeps the data inside in exact arithmetic, and rounding took a cell an ulp out – at |c| = 1, a one-cell shift only to within an ulp (see _validate_courant), a few ulps short of it, and where a plateau at a bound met rough data – which the next checked call refused: under fmax = 0.987, c = 1 took [0.195, 0.694, 0.566, 0.987] to a first cell of 0.9870000000000001. The clamp cuts that ulp, and the mass with it; a line inside is returned as the arithmetic gives it, to the bit. Another scheme's step leaves such ulps too, and the check takes them: round-off is _roundoff past a bound, and the check is _check_bounds. What it takes comes back clamped too, even at c = 0, so that round-off another scheme leaves does not build up through it from call to call. checked is a type parameter, so checked = false compiles both away for runs whose bounds are known good.

Vasilek.Advection.PFCNonUniform — Type
PFCNonUniform(Δx; fmin, fmax, checked = true)

Positive Flux Conservative scheme on a static non-uniform grid, Δx being the cell widths. Needs a workspace.

The limiter coefficient is computed per cell triple, so a refined region does not tighten the limiter elsewhere. fmin, fmax and checked are PFC's, with the same check and the same clamp.

advect! takes a displacement α = vΔt here, not a Courant number, since a non-uniform grid has none, and it is bounded by the narrowest cell: |α| ≤ minimum(Δx). Every cell gives up its outgoing flux alone, so a wider step is wrong in the narrowest cell however wide its neighbours are. OnGrid splits a longer step into sub-steps.

At |α| = minimum(Δx) the narrowest cells take a step of exactly one cell, which rounding took out of the bounds as it did PFC's: with fmin = 0 and data that touches it, α = 0.1 over fill(0.1, 6) took [0, 0.01268125, 0.01268125, 0, 0, 0.01268125] to -1.7e-18 in its second cell. A checked one clamps it to 0.

Vasilek.Advection.SemiLagrangian — Type

source
Vasilek.Advection.Superbee — Type

Superbee limiter [Roe, Annu. Rev. Fluid Mech. 18, 337 (1986)]: r -> max(0, min(2r, 1), min(r, 2)), the upper edge of Sweby's second-order total-variation-diminishing region and so the most compressive limiter that keeps Godunov with PiecewiseLinear second order and TVD up to |c| = 1.

Use it for sharp fronts. Its compression is anti-diffusion: on smooth data it steepens slopes, so the L² norm of a perturbation can grow, which biases a damping rate low in a Vlasov–Poisson run. For smooth phase-space dynamics prefer VanLeer or PFC. test/test_comparison.jl and verification/scheme-comparison.jl quantify both.

source
Vasilek.Advection.Upwind — Type

First-order upwind. Total-variation diminishing, first order.

source
Vasilek.Advection.VanLeer — Type

Van Leer limiter [Van Leer, J. Comput. Phys., 14 (4), 361 (1974)]: r -> (r + |r|)/(1 + |r|), which is 0 for r ≤ 0 and tends to 2 as r grows; it is 2 at r = Inf.

Vasilek.Advection._check_bounds — Method
_check_bounds(src, fmin, fmax)

The check PFC and PFCNonUniform run on every call when built with checked = true: that src lies inside the [fmin, fmax] their limiters are built on to round-off, or a DomainError. Both call this one function, so they refuse the same data with the same message. Inlined, so each scheme's if Checked removes it whole. An exception rather than @assert, which is a debugging aid that may be compiled out, and this is part of the schemes' contract. What a checked call returns it clamps into the bounds, _hold!.

Round-off is _roundoff past a bound, and only a line outside the bounds pays for it: one inside is the two comparisons it always was. Another scheme's step rounds a line past a bound at its own extremum, as one taken beside the driver's default does, and refusing that ulp stopped the run.

Vasilek.Advection._hold! — Method
_hold!(dest, src, l, u)

src clamped into [l, u], into dest, which may be src itself. l and u are the bounds already rounded inward into dest's type, by _inward, so that a narrower dest holds nothing past them; a checked PFC and PFCNonUniform round them before they write dest and clamp what they return with this. Comparisons and ifelse, so that it vectorizes, where clamp! cost a third of a PFC step at N = 10000.

Vasilek.Advection._nonuniform_at — Method
_nonuniform_at(Δz, scale)

The index of the first spacing in Δz that differs from the first by more than rounding, or nothing for a uniform grid: 1e-10 of the first spacing, or 8 ulps of scale in scale's own type, whichever is more. A spacing is the difference of two coordinates and carries their rounding, which scales with the coordinates, scale the largest of them, rather than with the spacing: Float32 coordinates up to 25 leave widths of π/8 4.9e-6 of themselves apart, Float64 ones near 1e6 widths of 0.1 1.2e-9 apart, and OnGrid refused both while it took 1e-12 of the spacing. make_poisson checks its nodes by the same rule, _within_rounding, and OnGrid a uniform scheme's widths with this. NaN is never within rounding.

Vasilek.Advection._prefilter! — Method
_prefilter!(ws, spline, src)

The B-spline coefficients of src, into ws.coefficients. The linear spline's are the data, and the first point again at n + 1.

The quadratic and cubic splines' coefficients solve M c = src, M tridiagonal with a on the diagonal and b off it and in the corners M[1,n], M[n,1]. With u = e₁ + eₙ, M = A + b·u·uᵀ, A tridiagonal with a − b in its corners and strictly diagonally dominant, and by Sherman–Morrison

c = y − b(y₁ + yₙ)·z,    y = A⁻¹src,    z = A⁻¹u/(1 + b·uᵀA⁻¹u).

The workspace holds A's factorisation (rdiag, reciprocal pivots) and z, so a step is two sweeps and an axpy.

Vasilek.Advection._roundoff — Method
_roundoff(F, scale)

How far past a bound a value of a line of type F is taken for round-off by a checked PFC or PFCNonUniform, where scale is the largest magnitude among it, its two neighbours either side and the bound: the values a scheme's stencil rounded to get there. 1024 ulps of F at that scale, 2.3e-13 of it for Float64 and as many ulps of BigFloat. Nothing that is not finite is round-off.

A line of a type coarser than Float64 is taken only inside its bounds, as before: no count of its ulps told round-off from data outside them. Godunov with a limiter left a Float32 line 9 ulps over its maximum and a Float16 one 18 within 64 OnGrid sub-steps, and 4 ulps took a Float16 line 0.3% below 0. The driver works its lines in Float64 at least, and never hands one over.

The scale is local, the bound being tested and the cell's neighbours, so that neither the far bound nor a peak elsewhere on the line widens it: a loose fmax = 1e300 took -5 for round-off past fmin = 0 while it counted, and a line's own peak would take a real undershoot of a cell beside zeros. The scale is taken in a float type both the line and the bound fit, so that a Float32 line under fmax = 1e300 does not overflow it and an integer line is judged in Float64.

A step leaves a Float64 line at most an ulp of that scale past them – Upwind, the constant Godunov and the linear SemiLagrangian – or about an ulp per sub-step of OnGrid, Godunov with a limiter, where a plateau at the bound meets values just below it: 111 ulps over 256 sub-steps and 197 over 1024 at worst, and none over 4096 or more, where the line has smoothed below the bound. None of these went below 0 at a jump to it.

Vasilek.Advection._validate — Method
_validate(dest, src, scheme, c, ws)

Argument check run at the top of every advect! method. Each of these used to give a plausible wrong answer instead of an error:

  • dest sharing memory with src (Base.mightalias, so a view counts): the stencils read neighbours of src that dest has already overwritten;
  • unequal lengths, fewer than three cells, or non-one-based indexing;
  • a workspace not exactly the one workspace(scheme, length(src)) returns (the spline prefilter runs over the whole buffer, so a longer one is as wrong as a shorter one, and a workspace built for another spline holds another spline's factorisation). A scheme that takes none now refuses any other, which it used to accept and ignore; see _validate_workspace;
  • a workspace narrower than the data, _validate_eltype: a step works the line in its workspace's type and rounded the data to it, 5.9e-8 off for Float64 data under a Float32 grid's PFCNonUniform;
  • a workspace whose buffers share memory with dest or src (Base.mightalias again): the spline prefilter overwrites its coefficients, which dest is then sampled from, and PFCNonUniform accumulates into its own while it still reads src;
  • a step the scheme cannot take; see _validate_courant.

The checks are comparisons outside the loop and allocate nothing; the error paths are @noinline.

Vasilek.Advection._validate_courant — Method
_validate_courant(scheme, c)

Refuse a step scheme cannot take: |c| > 1 for the schemes with a Courant limit, and for PFCNonUniform, whose c is a displacement, |c| wider than its narrowest cell. c = ±1 exactly is accepted; so is any finite c by SemiLagrangian, since characteristic tracing has no Courant limit. NaN and ±Inf are refused by every scheme.

Upwind, LaxWendroff, Godunov and PFC are explicit, and none survives a characteristic crossing more than one cell per step: past |c| = 1 round-off at the grid scale grows every step, slowly enough for the first steps to look right, and PFC loses positivity first. At |c| = 1 each is a one-cell shift to within an ulp of the larger of the two cells involved: every flux is then its upwind cell's whole value, and a cell takes f[i] + (f[i−1] − f[i]) – Upwind's f[i] − (f[i] − f[i−1]) is the same rounding – which is f[i−1] exactly only where the difference is exact, as it is between neighbours within a factor of two of each other. LaxWendroff rounds more terms. That ulp can take a cell outside a PFC's bounds, and a checked PFC or PFCNonUniform clamps it back.

The bounded method is the default, so a new scheme is checked unless it opts out, as SemiLagrangian does: one that forgets to opt out is refused loudly, where one that forgot to opt in would be wrong quietly.

Vasilek.Advection._validate_workspace — Method
_validate_workspace(scheme, ws, n)

Check that ws is what workspace(scheme, n) would have returned. Exact equality, not a lower bound: the spline prefilter runs over the whole buffer, so a longer one is as wrong as a shorter one. A spline's workspace also has to be its own spline's, since it holds that spline's factorisation.

A scheme takes nothing unless it has a method here for its own workspace, so a scheme that needs none refuses whatever else it is given: Upwind took another scheme's workspace without a word, which is how a sweep that mixes up its workspaces goes unnoticed until a scheme that reads one gets the wrong one. A new scheme with a workspace adds its method, and one to _scratch_aliases, or is refused loudly.

Vasilek.Advection.advect! — Method
advect!(dest, src, scheme, c[, ws])

Advance src one step into dest at Courant number c.

The four-argument form allocates a workspace of src's element type when the scheme needs one; pass one explicitly in any loop that runs more than once.

dest and src must be distinct arrays of equal length, at least three elements long; ws must be the workspace workspace(scheme, length(src)) returns; and |c| ≤ 1 unless the scheme is SemiLagrangian – for PFCNonUniform, whose c is a displacement, |c| ≤ minimum(Δx). Each of those is checked; see _validate for why.

Vasilek.Advection.nsubsteps — Method
nsubsteps(og::OnGrid, α)

How many equal sub-steps OnGrid takes the displacement α in: substeps(α, og.h), so that none crosses a cell, and 1 for a scheme with no Courant limit. SemiLagrangian has a method returning 1; a scheme of another type that has none adds one.

|α/m| ≤ h is what PFCNonUniform checks, and for a scheme that takes a Courant number it gives |(α/m)/h| ≤ 1, division being monotone. Where α fits a cell, |α| ≤ h exactly when |α/h| ≤ 1 after rounding, so m = 1 wherever the step alone would have been taken: Upwind at |α| = h is its own step at c = ±1.

Vasilek.Advection.substeps — Method
substeps(α, h)

The fewest equal parts of α that are each no longer than h, and one for an α that is not finite, which advect! then refuses itself.

A part of exactly h is a step of |c| = 1, which a checked PFC or PFCNonUniform clamps into its bounds: OnGrid(PFCNonUniform(fill(0.1, 6); fmin = 0, fmax = 1)) took α = 0.2 in two steps of exactly 0.1, and the second refused the -1.7e-18 the first had left in a cell that held 0, until the scheme clamped what it returned. For a while those two were given parts shorter than h instead, which a step a few ulps short of a cell still beat.

Vasilek.workspace — Function
workspace(scheme, n[, T])

Scratch memory for scheme at problem size n and element type T (Float64 unless the scheme carries its own), or nothing when it needs none. One workspace per task: that is what makes the schemes safe to run concurrently over the lines of a multidimensional sweep. advect! refuses one narrower than the data, which the step would round to it.
